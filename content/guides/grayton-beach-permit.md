# Grayton Beach Driving Permits

One of the most unique and sought-after experiences in Grayton Beach is the ability to drive your vehicle directly onto the sand. It’s a long-standing local tradition, but it’s also one that is strictly regulated to protect the beach ecosystem and ensure safety for everyone.

## The Lottery System

Because of the high demand and limited space, permits to drive on Grayton Beach are issued via an **annual lottery system** conducted by Walton County.

- **Who can apply?** The lottery is typically open to real property owners in Walton County.
- **When is the lottery?** Applications usually open in the spring.
- **How many permits?** The number of permits is limited and varies each year.

## Rules of the Sand

If you’re lucky enough to secure a permit, there are several rules you must follow:
1. **Permit Display:** Your permit must be clearly visible on your vehicle at all times while on the beach.
2. **Speed Limit:** The speed limit on the sand is very low (usually 5 mph) to protect pedestrians and wildlife.
3. **Designated Areas:** You can only drive and park in specific designated areas of the beach.
4. **Vehicle Requirements:** Only 4-wheel drive vehicles are allowed. Attempting to drive on the sand with 2-wheel drive is a guaranteed way to get stuck.

## Beach Access Points

The main vehicle access point for Grayton Beach is located at the end of **Hotz Avenue**. During peak times, there may be a queue to enter or exit the beach.

## For Visitors and Non-Permit Holders

If you don't have a permit, you cannot drive your vehicle onto the sand. However, you can still enjoy the beach by parking in the designated lots and walking down to the access. 

**Pro Tip:** If you want the beach driving experience without the permit, consider booking a local bonfire service or a chartered fishing trip (the "Grayton Beach Mosquito Fleet"), as these operators have the necessary access to launch from the sand.

## Why the Rules Matter

Grayton Beach is a delicate environment. The driving rules are in place to prevent dune erosion, protect sea turtle nesting sites, and ensure that the beach remains a safe place for families to play. Please respect the local regulations so that this unique tradition can continue for generations to come.
