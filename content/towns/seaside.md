## Seaside

Seaside is the town people mean when they talk about pastel cottages, airstream food, and a central amphitheater lawn. It was built as a walkable beach town before that phrase turned into marketing, and the layout still drives how people move: on foot, between green space, pavilions, and the beach.

## Snapshot on the map

The urban plan is the story. Narrow streets, shared lawns, and small retail pockets keep energy concentrated in a tight loop. Visitors mix with locals running errands, film-tourism curiosity, and families treating the town square like their living room for the week.

## What the week actually feels like

Peak weeks feel busy in the center even when the beach itself is manageable. Mornings can be calmer coffee runs and short walks before the square fills. Evenings rotate between casual takeout, sit-down dinners, and whatever is happening on the lawn if something is scheduled.

## The local pace right now

Seasonal events and holiday weeks amplify foot traffic. Shoulder season can feel almost sleepy by comparison. Parking and access stay recurring topics because the town is designed for walking once you are inside it, not for endless drop-off loops.

## Who tends to click with this area

First-timers who want the iconic 30A experience, families who like everything within a short walk, and anyone who enjoys people-watching as part of the vacation. If you dislike crowds in tight retail spaces, you may spend more time at the beach house than you expected.
